  * [The current bottleneck is political will, not research](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EexsebbYhbe2gXkPP/the-current-bottleneck-is-political-will-not-research)  
    * Concise case for this being the most important area to work on  
  * [An Activist View of AI Governance](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/4PLuwEyMZhT89Dxpo)  
    * Very useful series on US focused AI political advocacy  
  * [The Invisible Side of AI Governance](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AWKkDLDnShemNCSzZ/the-invisible-side-of-ai-governance)  
    * Overview of “insider” vs “outsider” political advocacy work  
  * [What We Learned from Briefing 140+ Lawmakers on the Threat from AI](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/A7BtBD9BAfK2kKSEr/what-we-learned-from-briefing-140-lawmakers-on-the-threat) (ControlAI)  
  * [Global Call for AI Red Lines](https://red-lines.ai/)  
    * Campaign for promoting international AI governance. The FAQ is useful for info on progress that's been made thus far and what still needs to happen in AI international governance.
  * [AI 2040](https://ai-2040.com/)  
    * Useful for thinking about how policy decisions fit into long-term future of AI development, although many of the proposals are likely too politically infeasible to aim for in practice.
  * [IFP’s AI safety low-regret policy recommendations](https://ifp.org/preparing-for-ai-research-automation/)  
    * AI policy proposals more feasible in the near-term.
  * [Stop Donating to AI Safety Research\*](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HuyZE6xFfebv7ByXC/stop-donating-to-ai-safety-research)  
    * Strong case for donating to political advocacy groups, has detailed information about several.
  * [Speaking to Congressional staffers about AI risk](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/2sLwt2cSAag74nsdN/speaking-to-congressional-staffers-about-ai-risk)  
    * Title is self-explanatory. This is from 2023 but still largely useful.
  * [Which AI harms & risks will mobilise the public to act?](https://www.socialchangelab.org/research/which-ai-harms-and-risks.html)
    * Many harms from AI have varying degrees of salience among the public and degree to which they are helpful for policy solutions that mitigate existential risk; this paper suggests autonomous warfare strikes the best balance and explores several others.
